Writing an Introduction

Formula: [Name] is famous around the world for + [main achievement].

✓ Effective
Marie Curie is famous around the world for her pioneering research into radioactivity.
✗ Too vague
Marie Curie was a woman who lived a long time ago.
It does not say why she is famous.
Narrating Events: Past Simple + Past Continuous

Main event: [Name] + Past simple + [main event].

Background: While + [Name] + was/were + V-ing, + [Name] + Past simple + [main event].

✓ Effective
While she was studying at the Sorbonne, she met a young scientist named Pierre Curie.
✗ Roles reversed
While she studied at the Sorbonne, she was meeting Pierre Curie.
Writing about Death and Legacy

Formula: [Name] died in [year], from/after [cause]. However, [legacy/continued impact].

✓ Effective
Marie Curie died in 1934, from an illness caused by years of exposure to radiation. Her achievements, however, continue to save lives today.
✗ Incomplete
Then she died.
It gives no lasting impact.

Complete Model Biography

Marie Curie is famous around the world for her pioneering research into radioactivity. She was born in Warsaw, Poland, in 1867. While working as a governess to help support her family, she continued studying science in secret. In 1891, she moved to Paris to study at the Sorbonne. While she was studying there, she met a young scientist named Pierre Curie, and the couple married in 1895. Marie Curie was an extraordinarily successful scientist. Together with her husband, she discovered two new elements, polonium and radium. She was also famous for winning two Nobel Prizes, one in Physics in 1903 and one in Chemistry in 1911, becoming the first person ever to win a Nobel Prize in two different sciences. Marie Curie had two daughters, Irène and Ève. While raising her daughters, she was also running her own research laboratory in Paris. After Pierre died suddenly in 1906, she carried on their work alone and later trained Irène, who also became a Nobel Prize winner. Marie Curie died in 1934, from an illness caused by years of exposure to radiation. Her achievements, however, continue to save lives today, as the treatment she pioneered is still used in hospitals worldwide.

Quick Recap

A clear biography follows a meaningful timeline, not a dry list of dates.

Combine sources, distinguish main events from background, and end with lasting impact.
